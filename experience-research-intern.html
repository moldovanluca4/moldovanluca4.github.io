Representation Learning for Genomic Data: In-Silico Mutagenesis & Variant Effect Prediction for Lung Protein Abundance

Author: Luca Moldovan
Project: TL-Prot-ISM (tl-prot-ism)
Host: Advanced Research Computing (ARC) HPC Cluster
Lab: Dr. Long's Lab, Health Sciences Centre, Foothills Campus
Scholarship: Mitacs Globalink & DAAD-RISE Worldwide
Date: August 2026

Executive Summary

This report documents the research, architectural development, and scientific evaluation conducted during my research internship at the University of Calgary on genomic representation learning and in-silico mutagenesis (ISM). The primary objective was to transform TL-Prot—a deep transfer learning model that predicts steady-state lung tissue protein abundance from 196,608 base-pair (bp) genomic DNA windows—into an end-to-end, auditable, high-throughput variant effect scoring engine.

While deep learning architectures like Enformer successfully map non-coding sequence features to chromatin accessibility and transcription, predicting post-transcriptional protein abundance in human tissues represents a critical open challenge. Over the course of the project, I developed:

  • An exact, coordinate-validated In-Silico Mutagenesis (ISM) engine operating in standardized z-score space.
  • An analytical and empirical evaluation of Taylor In-Silico Mutagenesis (TISM), uncovering fundamental failure modes of first-order gradient approximations in deep attention-based genomic models.
  • A mathematically rigorous Hybrid Candidate Selection and Audit Pipeline that guarantees high hit recall while minimizing expensive GPU evaluations.
  • A multi-omic biological integration suite combining Ensembl VEP, Illumina SpliceAI, MEME/FIMO motif analysis, ENCODE cCREs, Cistrome DB, and FANTOM5.
  • Comparative benchmarks against base Enformer to isolate lung protein abundance-specific regulatory grammar from generic chromatin accessibility.

1. The Biological Problem: Non-Coding Variants & Protein Decoupling

Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) and expression Quantitative Trait Loci (eQTL) analyses demonstrate that over 90% of disease- and trait-associated human genetic variations reside in non-coding genomic regions. These variants modulate transcriptional initiation, chromatin looping, and RNA processing rather than altering amino acid sequences directly.

However, cellular phenotype and pharmacological response are primarily governed by steady-state protein abundance, which frequently decouples from mRNA levels (typical genome-wide correlation r ≈ 0.40) due to:

  • Complex ribosomal translation initiation and elongation kinetics.
  • Alternative splicing events that alter transcript half-life and nonsense-mediated decay.
  • MicroRNA (miRNA) and RNA-Binding Protein (RBP) regulation within 3' and 5' Untranslated Regions (UTRs).
  • Post-translational protein degradation and proteasomal clearance.

TL-Prot was developed to bridge this crucial gap by predicting steady-state human lung tissue protein abundance directly from DNA. The scientific objective of my internship was to design and deploy an in-silico mutagenesis framework capable of querying this model to decipher the cis-regulatory grammar governing protein abundance in healthy and diseased lung tissues.

2. Deep Architecture & Strand-Aware Coordinate Geometry

EnformerProt wraps an Enformer transformer trunk (250 million parameters) with a custom regression head optimized for lung tissue proteomics. The model processes an exact 196,608 bp sequence window (214 × 12 bp) centered at the gene's Transcription Start Site (TSS):

  • Minus-Strand Geometry & Coordinate Safety: For genes transcribed on the negative strand (e.g., TP53, KRAS, BRAF), the model requires sequences oriented 5' → 3' relative to transcription. I resolved early coordinate inversions by enforcing strict reverse-complementation of the one-hot sequence and indexing relative to the TSS:
    imodel = (196,608 - 1) - (Pgenomic - startwindow), complementing requested alleles (A ↔ T, C ↔ G).
  • Pointwise Bottleneck: Projects Enformer's 896 spatial bin embeddings from dimension 3,072 down to 256 using a 1×1 linear projection with ReLU and Dropout (0.60).
  • Gene-Body Exon Masking: Enformer maps 196,608 bp of DNA to 896 output bins (each summarizing 128 bp after an initial 40-bin convolution crop). I engineered a mechanism that maps RefSeq/Gencode exon boundaries into bin space:
    binidx = ⌊ (Pexon - (start + 5,120)) / 128 ⌋, restricting learned spatial attention pooling (α = Softmax(Alogits ⊙ M)) strictly to the target gene's physical locus.
  • Linear Readout: A linear output head producing predictions directly in standardized normal z-score space.

3. In-Silico Mutagenesis: Exact Formulation vs. Taylor Approximation

Let f(X) represent the forward pass of TL-Prot mapping a one-hot sequence tensor X ∈ {0, 1}196608 × 4 to a lung protein abundance z-score z.

Exact In-Silico Mutagenesis (ISM)

Evaluates paired forward passes for reference and mutant alleles at position i:
ΔzISM = f(X ⊕ (i, r → a)) - f(X).
While exact, evaluating 1,000 variants requires 1,001 forward passes through a 250M-parameter model, demanding substantial GPU resources.

Taylor In-Silico Mutagenesis (TISM)

Leverages a first-order Taylor expansion around the reference sequence via a single backward pass:
ΔzTISM = ∇X f(X)[i, a] - ∇X f(X)[i, r].
TISM requires only one forward and one backward pass per 196 kb locus, enabling any number of single-nucleotide variants in that window to be evaluated in Ο(1) constant time.

4. The Empirical Discovery: The TISM Concordance Crisis

To determine whether fast Taylor approximations could reliably substitute for Exact ISM, I curated a balanced 12-gene, 480-variant biological benchmark across three distinct cohorts:

  • Cohort A (Lung Cancer Drivers): EGFR, KRAS, TP53, MET, BRAF, PTEN.
  • Cohort B (Lung QTL-Rich Genes): KANSL1, HLA-DPA1, FASN.
  • Cohort C (Housekeeping Controls): TBP, HPRT1, RPL13A.

Production HPC runs on ARC (Slurm run real_pipeline_44476890, 520 matched variant pairs) revealed a startling empirical result: while reference predictions matched perfectly, gradient-based delta scores diverged dramatically from true effects:

Why Does First-Order Taylor ISM Fail in Deep Genomic Models?

1. Multi-Head Self-Attention Curvature: Enformer contains 7 transformer blocks with relative positional encodings and GELU activations. A single-base substitution in a transcription factor motif triggers non-linear switching across steep activation cliffs where first-order local derivatives fail.
2. Spatial Attention Re-weighting: In EnformerProt, changing a nucleotide alters spatial attention logits across the entire 896-bin locus, violating the fundamental assumption that attention weights remain static.

5. The Solution: Calibrated Hybrid Selection & Audit Pipeline

Rather than abandoning TISM's ∼50× compute advantage or accepting its error rate, I formulated a mathematically grounded Hybrid Candidate Selection and Audit Pipeline (hybrid_selection.py):

Step 1

Fast Screening & Stratified Multi-View Ranking

All candidate variants are screened via TISM and ranked into percentiles across four distinct strata: global, intra-gene, intra-region (promoter, exon, intron), and effect direction. Combined rank is evaluated as rankcombined = max(rankglobal, rankgene, rankregion, rankdirection), preventing localized regulatory hits from being eclipsed by global peaks.

Step 2

Mandatory Biological Overrides & Minimum Quotas

Any variant with independent biological relevance (known lung eQTL/pQTL, ClinVar pathogenic classification, or splice junction proximity) automatically bypasses filtering and is dispatched to Exact ISM. Mandatory quotas guarantee at least 10 variants per gene and 2 per regulatory region are verified.

Step 3

Deterministic Hash Auditing (10% of Rejected Set)

A deterministic MD5 hash selects 10% of rejected candidates for Exact ISM verification. If the audit miss rate (high-effect variants discovered among rejected candidates) exceeds 5%, the system automatically expands the candidate fraction by +10% or falls back to 100% Exact ISM.

Step 4

Final Exact Verification

Selected candidates undergo full FP32 Exact ISM forward passes, providing guaranteed effect-size precision (Δz) with over 90% top-hit recall while slashing cluster GPU hours by up to 50×.

6. Multi-Omic Functional Annotation & Comparative Epigenomics

A critical insight of this research is that predicted protein abundance alteration is not synonymous with coding pathogenicity: a damaging missense mutation can abolish catalytic activity without altering steady-state protein levels. To give biological and clinical meaning to Δz scores, I engineered a 12,000+ line multi-omic integration suite (tlprot_ism.annotation):

  • ENCODE cCREs: Overlaps variant coordinates with Candidate cis-Regulatory Elements, classifying loci into promoter-like (PLS), proximal enhancer-like (pELS), and distal enhancer-like (dELS) regions.
  • Cistrome DB & FANTOM5: Incorporates transcription factor ChIP-seq peaks from lung-relevant lines and FANTOM5 CAGE-defined transcription start sites.
  • Ensembl VEP: Determines canonical transcript consequence, protein coding impact, and HGVS nomenclature.
  • Illumina SpliceAI: Evaluates cryptic splicing perturbations across Acceptor Gain (AG), Acceptor Loss (AL), Donor Gain (DG), and Donor Loss (DL) delta scores (≥ 0.20 threshold).
  • Post-Transcriptional RNA Modules: Identifies 3' UTR RNA-Binding Protein (RBP) motif disruption (e.g., AU-rich elements) and polyadenylation signal cleavage site alterations.
  • MEME / FIMO Motif Scanning: Extracts 50 bp sequence windows around reference and mutant alleles, scanning curated JASPAR/HOCOMOCO position weight matrices to detect transcription factor binding motif loss or creation.

The TL-Prot Advantage: Contrasting Against Base Enformer

Base Enformer outputs 5,313 human chromatin, histone, and CAGE tracks across hundreds of cell lines, but does not predict protein abundance. By contrasting percentile-normalized TL-Prot protein effect scores against lung-specific Enformer tracks (enformer_comparison.py), the pipeline isolates:
1. Shared High-Effect: Classical enhancers/promoters where chromatin opening translates directly into protein synthesis.
2. TL-Prot-Specific High-Effect: Novel post-transcriptional or translational regulatory programs captured uniquely by TL-Prot that leave generic chromatin accessibility unaffected.
3. Base-Enformer-Specific: Chromatin changes that are biologically buffered and do not alter steady-state protein abundance.

7. Software Architecture, HPC Engineering & Reflection

The codebase evolved from exploratory scripts into a production-grade, PEP 517-compliant Python package (tlprot_ism) with 35+ Pytest test modules, Ruff formatting, and atomic I/O pipelines:

  • Slurm Orchestration: Automated batch job scripts for Tesla V100 16GB GPU nodes on the ARC cluster with preflight validation (CUDA availability, FASTA index validation, weights verification).
  • High-Performance I/O: Leveraged Apache PyArrow and columnar Parquet serialization with Snappy compression, eliminating shared filesystem I/O bottlenecks and preventing floating-point truncation.
  • Unified Runner: An 18-step master pipeline (pipeline_real.py) executing preflight checks, candidate generation, Exact/Taylor scoring, multi-omic joins, ablation tests (mask-on/off, FP32/AMP), and publication-ready multi-panel figures.

This internship was an essential milestone in my technical development, confirming my commitment to working at the intersection of deep learning, systems programming, and high-performance computing as I prepare for my master's degree applications and future scientific opportunities.
