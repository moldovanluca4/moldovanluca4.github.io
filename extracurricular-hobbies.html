Esports & Systems Foundations

From Counter-Strike to Computer Architecture

I have always been deeply passionate about video games. In high school, that passion turned into serious competitive ambition: I set out to break into the professional Counter-Strike scene, competing in tournaments for Atomic Esports. Dedicated scrims, tactical coordination, and intense rounds demanded fast reflexes, split-second decision-making, and an analytical mindset to dissect performance and eliminate mistakes.

Competing at that level naturally sparked an obsession with PC components and peripherals. When every millisecond of input latency and every frame counts, you quickly develop a deep appreciation for mechanical switch actuation, high-polling sensors, monitor refresh rates, and hardware optimizations. Researching, configuring, and assembling custom gaming rigs evolved into a craft of its own.

Studying PC components to build gaming machines ended up indirectly serving as my first real education in computer architecture. Evaluating hardware taught me how CPUs, GPUs, memory buses, PCIe topologies, and storage hierarchies relate and interact to form a cohesive system without bottlenecks. Once the hardware clicked, curiosity naturally expanded to the next layer: the operating system, kernel interactions, device drivers, process scheduling, and the software stack that drives the machine.
