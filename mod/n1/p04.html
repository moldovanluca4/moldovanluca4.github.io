PLOS Genetics · 2026 Transfer Learning

Tissue-specific transfer learning improves functional variant and therapeutic target discoveries in breast and prostate cancer

Qing Li, Dinghao Wang, Zilong Zhang, Deshan Perera, Zhishan Chen, Wanqing Wen, M. Ethan MacDonald, Weijia Cai, Jun Yan, Xiao-Ou Shu, Wei Zheng, Xingyi Guo & Quan Long

Nature Methods · 2021 Enformer

Effective gene expression prediction from sequence by integrating long-range interactions (Enformer)

Žiga Avsec, Vikram Agarwal, Daniel Visentin, Joseph R. Ledsam, Agnieszka Grabska-Barwinska et al.

Introduced the 250M-parameter Enformer transformer architecture integrating cis-regulatory interactions across 196,608 bp of DNA. Studying its 896-bin spatial pooling geometry and 5,313 human chromatin and CAGE tracks was essential to building EnformerProt.

Nature Reviews Genetics · 2012 Protein Abundance

Insights into the regulation of protein abundance from proteomic and transcriptomic analyses

Christine Vogel & Edward M. Marcotte

Explains why steady-state cellular protein abundance frequently decouples from mRNA transcript levels (r ≈ 0.40) due to translation kinetics, 5’/3’ UTR regulation, and protein degradation—framing the biological motivation behind TL-Prot-ISM.

Comprehensive proteogenomic characterization of 110 human lung adenocarcinoma tumors and matched normal adjacent lung tissues, providing the biological context for our lung proteomics cohorts and driver genes (KRAS, EGFR, TP53).

Algorithmic Genomics Taylor ISM

Approximating In-Silico Saturation Mutagenesis Using First-Order Taylor Series Expansions

Alexander Sasse, Maria Chikina & Sara Mostafavi

Formulates how first-order Taylor series expansions approximate in-silico mutagenesis (ΔzTISM) from a single backward pass. Studying this algorithm led directly to my TISM vs. Exact ISM concordance study and my calibrated Hybrid Selection & Audit Pipeline.

Combines AlphaFold structural context with evolutionary conservation to predict coding missense pathogenicity across the human proteome, contrasting coding structural damage against non-coding cis-regulatory protein abundance changes.
