Projects / Readings & Academic Foundation

Books, research papers, technical resources, and bachelor’s coursework behind my projects.

Every project I have built—from deep genomic sequence modeling and enterprise mainframe integration to C++/Qt desktop software, Rust system-inspection tools, and self-hosted Linux infrastructure—grew out of focused reading, primary literature, documentation deep-dives, and university coursework.

This page brings together the books, scientific papers, engineering resources, and completed bachelor’s courses at Constructor University (B.Sc. in Computer Science with a Minor in Mathematics) that helped me develop my understanding and turn theoretical concepts into working systems.

01 / Foundational Literature

Books That Shaped My Engineering & Systems Mindset

Book · Systems & Architecture Mainframe Integration & OS

Computer Systems: A Programmer’s Perspective (CS:APP)

Randal E. Bryant & David R. O’Hallaron

A foundational text connecting hardware architecture, representation, machine-level execution, memory hierarchies, linking, virtual memory, and system-level I/O. It shaped how I reason about what happens underneath high-level code when a C executable interacts with the operating system and higher-level Python layers.

02 / Scientific Literature

Research Papers Behind My Genomics & Deep Learning Work

Nature Methods · 2021 TL-Prot-ISM Research

Effective gene expression prediction from sequence by integrating long-range interactions (Enformer)

Žiga Avsec, Vikram Agarwal, Daniel Visentin, Joseph R. Ledsam, Agnieszka Grabska-Barwinska et al.

Introduced the Enformer transformer architecture capable of integrating regulatory information across 196,608 bp of DNA. Studying this paper and its 896-bin spatial pooling geometry was essential to wrapping the 250M-parameter trunk in EnformerProt, implementing gene-body exon masking, and benchmarking lung protein abundance predictions against 5,313 base chromatin and CAGE tracks.

EnformerGenomic Transformers196 kb ContextSelf-AttentionEpigenomics
Cell · 2019 TL-Prot-ISM Annotation Suite

Predicting splicing from primary sequence with deep learning (SpliceAI)

Kishore Jaganathan, Sofia Kyriazopoulou Panagiotopoulou, Jeremy F. McRae, Siavash Fazel Darbandi et al.

Demonstrated how deep residual networks predict cryptic splice-site creation and disruption directly from pre-mRNA sequence. This paper informed the post-transcriptional splicing module in my 12,000-line multi-omic annotation pipeline, using Acceptor/Donor Gain and Loss delta scores to explain non-coding variants that alter steady-state protein abundance via transcript stability.

Illumina SpliceAICryptic SplicingPost-Transcriptional RegulationVariant Interpretation
NeurIPS & Attribution Literature ISM vs. Taylor ISM

Self-Attention Curvature & First-Order Gradient Attribution in Deep Genomic Models

Ashish Vaswani et al. (2017) · Genomic Saliency & Taylor In-Silico Mutagenesis Literature

Combining transformer self-attention theory with first-order gradient attribution methods motivated our empirical comparison of Exact In-Silico Mutagenesis (ΔzISM) against Taylor ISM (ΔzTISM). Understanding non-linear GELU cliffs and spatial attention re-weighting across 896 bins explained why first-order gradients missed top regulatory hits and led directly to my calibrated Hybrid Selection & Audit Pipeline.

In-Silico MutagenesisTaylor ApproximationAttention CurvatureHybrid Auditing

ENCODE cCREs, Ensembl VEP & MEME/FIMO Motif Scanning

The ENCODE Project Consortium (2020) · William McLaren et al. (2016) · Charles E. Grant, Timothy L. Bailey & William S. Noble (2011)

Provided the biological annotation framework for mapping high-effect variants to Candidate cis-Regulatory Elements (promoters PLS, proximal/distal enhancers pELS/dELS), canonical transcript consequences via Ensembl VEP, and transcription factor binding site gain/loss across JASPAR and HOCOMOCO position weight matrices using FIMO.
